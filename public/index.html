Before your first run on a new mount, open Calibrate to align the capture window to where the card actually lands in frame — recognition matches a cropped photo against each card's reference image, so a tight, consistent crop still matters even though recognition itself now runs locally (5/6 correct in the last real test).

The recognition step matches your capture against every card in the selected set(s) — an unscoped run would mean matching against the whole game's catalog, which testing showed doesn't work reliably (see the recognition-layer rewrite plan). Select every set in the pile if it's mixed.

The app can't detect foil or condition from the photo — pre-split your pile by variant before running, and declare it here (see plan Decision #4).

Advanced — confidence thresholds

Cosine similarity in [-1, 1], not the old 0-100 scale — real card photos typically score ~0.4-0.95. Any card below either threshold routes to bulk with a searchable record (Decision #2/#9). Defaults come from a 6-photo test run, not a full calibration — tune from a real dry run's manifest.csv.

Aligns the capture window to where the card sits in your fixed mount — a one-time setup per rig, not per run. Every capture is cropped to this box before it's sent for recognition.

Adjust until a card placed in the holder fills the dashed box, then Save. The box is always centered left-to-right.

Pixels reaching recognition

The model resizes whatever it gets so its shortest edge is 224px, then keeps only the centre 224×224 square. So the shortest edge of the capture window is what actually sets recognition detail — and anything outside that centre square is discarded before the model sees it. Ratios under about 1.5× mean marginally-sampled pixels that no threshold tuning can recover.
